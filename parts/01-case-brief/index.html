Part 1: Case brief

Aspelia Markets Ltd: a multi-asset online trading platform in Limassol, a regulator's findings letter, and the data management programme that follows.

Fictional case. Aspelia Markets Ltd, its people, systems, figures and the inspection described here are invented. Any resemblance to a real firm is coincidental. All data used in later parts is synthetic.

1. The company

Legal entityAspelia Markets Ltd, Limassol, Cyprus
LicenceCyprus Investment Firm (CIF), authorised by the Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission (CySEC) under Law 87(I)/2017, which transposes MiFID II. Passported to other EEA countries.
BusinessOnline multi-asset trading platform: real shares and ETFs, plus CFDs on indices, currencies and commodities. Web and mobile apps.
ClientsAbout 42,000 funded accounts, mostly retail, in 19 EEA countries; a small number of professional clients.
PeopleAbout 180 staff across Trading Operations, Client Onboarding, Compliance and AML, Finance, Marketing, Customer Support and IT.
Founded2016; grew quickly from 2020 by adding products and countries.

3. The trigger

In 2026 CySEC carried out an on-site inspection focused on anti-money-laundering controls. It sampled 60 client files. In 14 of them, at least one required KYC (Know Your Customer) item was missing, expired or did not match the client record: proof of address older than allowed, no source-of-funds evidence for large deposits, or a date of birth in the trading platform that differed from the ID document.

The findings letter gave Aspelia a deadline to remediate its client records and to show that the controls behind them work. The board appointed a Head of Data Governance and funded a data management programme. Its first question was a simple one: how many active clients do we have, and is their data complete? No one in the firm could answer it.

4. What the first weeks uncovered

The KYC gaps were a symptom. Looking for their causes turned up six related problems:

#ProblemExample
P1Incomplete and inconsistent client dataMissing KYC documents and expiry dates; countries stored as free text (“Germany”, “DE”, “Deutschland”).
P2Duplicate clientsThe same person registered twice, once from the web and once from the app, with two trading accounts and two risk ratings.
P3No one owns the dataWhen a client's address is wrong, Onboarding, Support and IT each say it is another team's job to fix.
P4Different meanings for the same word“Active client” means traded in 90 days for Trading, funded balance above zero for Finance, and logged in within 30 days for Marketing. The board pack mixes all three.
P5Nobody knows where data lives or how it flowsA GDPR access request took 40 days instead of the one month allowed, because nobody had a list of the systems holding the client's personal data.
P6Reporting built by handRegulatory reports are rejected when client identifiers are missing, and the board pack takes four days of manual work each month.

5. The programme

The programme follows the DAMA-DMBOK2 framework, which defines data management as the development, execution and supervision of plans, policies, programs and practices that deliver, control, protect and enhance the value of data and information assets throughout their lifecycles. Each part of this portfolio is one workstream:

6. How success is measured

  • KYC completeness for active clients: from an estimated 77% to at least 99%.
  • Duplicate client rate: below 0.5% of client records.
  • Every critical data element has a named owner and steward.
  • One approved definition of “active client”, used in every report.
  • GDPR access requests answered within the one-month limit, using the data inventory.
  • The monthly board pack produced from the warehouse, without manual rework.

7. Scope

In scope: client, account, instrument, trade and payment data across the systems above. Out of scope: market-data feeds, HR and payroll data, and the firm's own treasury.
